## How to read this / observations

The **classification columns are objective** (same math as the backend) — check those first. The
**explanation is the model's prose** — that's what to judge for quality.

- **The classifier ladder is correct across the board.** Negligible→Best/Excellent (B1, B7), slight→Good
  (B2), ~1 pawn→Mistake (B4, `b5`, swing ~1.7 pawn), piece/queen drops→Blunder (B5, B6). The win%
  numbers and categories are trustworthy.
- **Book override works as designed.** Every established-theory move the engine dislikes is re-graded
  **Book**: the three corpus lines (A1–A3), the Latvian Gambit (B8, engine *Mistake*), and even a mild
  *Inaccuracy* (B3 `Bd6`, established 2661 games). Only B4 `b5` stays a **Mistake** because it's *rare*
  (3 games) — i.e. genuinely not book. This is the intended behavior, and B4 is the clean "played a bad
  move that isn't theory" case.
- **Insight worth knowing:** a clean *non-book* Inaccuracy is nearly impossible to manufacture — a move
  slightly-bad-but-playable has usually been tried 25+ times by strong players, so it counts as
  established and gets the Book label. Non-book bad moves tend to jump straight to Mistake/Blunder.
- **Corpus explanations do use the book (A1–A3), but the 8B model misattributes it.** A1 credits the
  commentary to "Bent Larsen" and invents a Panjwani quote; the *right chunk* was retrieved (shown under
  "Book commentary the coach had"), but `llama3.1:8b` garbles the attribution and occasionally the side
  to move. That's a model-size limitation, not a retrieval/pipeline bug — a larger model would tighten it.
- **Blunder explanations are honest** (B5/B6): "not in the opening database … judged on the engine
  evaluation … Blunder." Exactly the intended framing for an off-book bad move.
